1 Introduction
Object detection is fundamental to autonomous driving
safety, particularly the reliable identiﬁcation of vulnerable
road users like pedestrians. Early physical adversarial at-
tacks aimed to make object detectors miss detecting people
by attempting on-object modiﬁcations, such as attaching ad-
versarial patches to clothing. However, physically tamper-
ing with individuals is highly impractical and conspicuous,
making such attack vectors unscalable and unrealistic for
widespread disruption. This limitation led subsequent re-
search to propose remote patch attacks, where an adversarial
pattern is placed away from potential targets on surfaces like
walls, billboards, or road surfaces, allowing a single patch to
aﬀect the detection of multiple objects, including pedestrians,
simultaneously.
The current state-of-the-art defense against the remote
patch attacks is ObjectSeeker [1], which oﬀers certiﬁably ro-
bust object detection. Its core idea is patch-agnostic masking:
ObjectSeeker systematically masks portions of the image and
runs a standard detector on each view. By aggregating results
with a certiﬁable rule, it guarantees a correct detection. This
is possible because at least one masked view will hide the
adversarial patch while retaining the object, thereby provid-
ing a formal certiﬁcation of robustness against patch attacks
within a deﬁned threat model.
This paper introduces Adversarial Background, a new re-
mote patch attack that nulliﬁes ObjectSeeker’s masking strat-
egy. Rather than optimising a small, localised patch, we learn
an adversarial texture that covers an entire planar surface,
such as a wall, signboard, or LED display, so every sliding
window inevitably contains part of the pattern. Expectation-
over-Transformation and total-variation regularisation are in-
corporated to maintain robustness against changes in view-
point, illumination, and print noise, enabling reliable failure
of state-of-the-art certiﬁed defences in real and simulated

Although the proposed attack is agnostic to the object class,
we instantiate and evaluate it on person detection as a repre-
sentative case study. Using YOLOv5s on the VOC dataset,
Adversarial Background lowers person-detection recall by
43 percentage points relative to the clean baseline and still
achieves a 35 percentage-point reduction when ObjectSeeker
is enabled. In a physical experiment with a 70” monitor
positioned behind a person standing in front of it, the at-
tack prevents detection in 70% of video frames under varied
lighting conditions, conﬁrming its real-world practicality.

Setup. From the VOC 2012 test set we collect 1,286 images
that contain the person class and divide them equally into
training and test subsets. Both the patch training and its eval-
uation are performed in a white-box setting with YOLOv5s.
The weighting factor for the total-variation term, 𝛼 , is em-

4 Future Work
In future work we will (i) carry out a comprehensive bench-
mark that compares Adversarial Backgrounds with a wide
range of state-of-the-art attacks and defences under iden-
tical settings; (ii) systematically clarify the conditions and
constraints required for the attack to succeed, including strict
black-box access, transferability across detector architectures,
and diverse real-world scenes; and (iii) validate the approach
on a complete autonomous-driving platform to conﬁrm its
impact on real vehicles.
